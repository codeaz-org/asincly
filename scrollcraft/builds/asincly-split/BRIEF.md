# BRIEF — Asincly landing v2 (Split stage)

**Self-authored under explicit creative delegation.** The user's words: "make the
landing page nice using that skill, make it unique and to stand out." Creative
direction delegated; product facts come from the repo (CLAUDE.md, docs/PLAN.md)
and the previous interviewed brief.

## 1. Vibe (3-5 words + refs)

Tense, then quiet. Cold vs warm.

- A chess clock: two sides, one shared time, pressure made visible.
- The office lights turning off at the end of a workday, floor by floor.
- A tug-of-war photograph, frozen mid-pull.

## 2. Scroll journey, section by section

- Open on a screen split exactly in half. Left: a cold, fluorescent world, "The
  standup at 09:30." Right: a warm pre-dawn world, "The standup whenever you
  wake." Both readable at once. The visitor understands the format before
  scrolling.
- The same workday advances on both sides simultaneously. Left: the calendar
  math of one meeting across ten time zones (03:30 for Sydney, computed, not
  claimed). Right: one teammate records two minutes in her own morning.
- The meeting itself runs on the left, timer ticking, ten people held, one
  talking. On the right, AI turns the recording into bullets, an action item, a
  flagged blocker.
- What remains: left, notes nobody opens and "wait, what did Sam say?". Right,
  the finished digest, read in seconds, blocker routed. The divider starts
  leaning right's way.
- A breath. Both sides go quiet.
- The collapse: the divider sweeps to the left edge. The meeting world crushes
  into a sliver; the morning takes the whole screen. This is the peak.
- Close inside the warm world: "Kill the meeting. Keep the standup." One CTA.
  Trust facts in one quiet line. The sync sliver stays at the edge, a tombstone.

## 3. Energy curve

Medium (tension of the split) → rising irritation on the left while the right
stays level and calm → a beat of near-silence → one big release (the collapse)
→ quiet, resolved. The page's loudness lives on the left side; the right side
never raises its voice, and that contrast is the argument.

## 4. Feeling curve + the one moment

Stage by stage: tension → recognition → irritation (left) against ease (right)
→ clarity → held breath → **relief** → resolve.

**The peak:** the collapse. After a whole page of the two worlds pulling at each
other, the divider finally gives and the meeting side crumples into a sliver
while the warm morning floods the full width.

## 5. One thing no site they have seen does

The divider is alive. It is the nav, the scorekeeper and a rope: the visitor
can grab it and drag the meeting world wider, and the page pulls back and snaps
to where the argument stands. Every beat passed stamps a mark on it. No landing
page lets you physically fight its thesis.

## 6. Range

Editorial-brutalist hybrid: hard 50/50 structure, hairline chrome, two grounds
held for the whole page (cold light left, warm dark right), one accent hue
(sunrise amber) at two lightnesses, keyed per ground. Not premium-minimal-dark
again (the previous build owns that), not playful.

## 7. One unbroken world, or distinct scenes?

Neither: two worlds held in tension on one pinned stage for the entire page.
The only "cut" is the collapse, and it is the point.

## 8. Assets

None generated (no API key, and none needed). Everything is real markup: the
calendar, the meeting roster, the timer, the digest, all computed in the page
from one demo-team array and labelled as a demo scenario. The restraint is the
aesthetic; the honesty rule from live-surface applies to every panel.
